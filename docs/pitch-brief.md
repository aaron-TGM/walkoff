## 1. Rules for anything you make

### Truth

1. **Only claim what's true today.** Roadmap items keep their badge
   (section 13). Never put them in the present tense.
2. **Invent nothing.** No customer logos, quotes, testimonials, case
   studies, brand or user counts, revenue, growth, benchmarks, market sizes,
   partners, press or investors that aren't in this file. No countdowns,
   "spots left" or other scarcity.
3. **Label examples as examples:** "Sample brand", "Sample campaign",
   "Example". Label the one real result with its brand, "Early test" and its
   source (section 8).
4. **Count customers by place, not passion.** "976 customers in Chargers
   territory", never "976 Chargers fans". Walkoff knows where customers
   live, not who they root for.
5. **No performance promises.** Don't say Walkoff "converts", "lifts revenue
   by X%", "drives sales" or "guarantees" anything. The only result is Frost
   Buddy's early test, stated the way section 8 states it.
6. **No legal promises.** The copy rules lower the risk for brands without a
   license. They aren't legal advice, so never write "legally safe",
   "risk-free", "compliant" or "cleared by the leagues".
7. **No affiliations.** Walkoff isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or
   sponsored by any league, team or player. Put that line on anything that
   names teams. Klaviyo, Shopify and Anthropic aren't partners either:
   Walkoff "works with Klaviyo and Shopify" and "runs in Claude". Never
   "partner", "official", "certified" or "backed by".

### Teams and athletes

8. **No team or league marks.** No logos, mascots, jerseys, uniforms,
   trophies or game footage, on any slide, for any audience. Show team
   colors, and write team names in plain text. The one exception is Frost
   Buddy's product photos (section 8): they're its licensed products, shown
   the way the site shows them.
9. **Sample creative stays unlicensed.** A mock email, ad or post written as
   a brand without a license never names the team, league, players, coaches,
   stadium, rivalry or event ("the big game", never "the Super Bowl"), and
   never shows a score. It nods with the colors, and the city for pro teams,
   through the brand's own products. Team and event names are fine in
   explanation written for marketers or investors: "When the Chargers win,
   Walkoff..."
10. **Never name an athlete,** in anything, investor decks included.
    Explanation can describe one the way the site does: "A quarterback from
    San Clemente plays for the Seahawks." Sample creative can't even do
    that. It celebrates the town, with no name, number, position, school or
    photo.

### Words

11. **Lead with the email and the money, not the tech.** The buyer is a
    marketer. Say what they get: a campaign, ready, with their own products
    in the team's colors, for their customers. Claude is where the work
    happens, not the pitch: no "AI-powered", "revolutionary", "supercharge"
    or "unlock". MCP, tool names and code belong only in technical material.
12. **One call to action:** "Get early access". Section 16 has the links.
13. **No sports clichés about marketing:** "slam dunk", "home run ROI",
    "game-changer". Use sports words precisely: walk-off, buzzer beater,
    derby, final whistle.
14. **Never write these.** Most were on the site once, and weren't true.

| Don't write | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "works with any AI agent", "every agent" | Only Claude and Claude Code are documented | "Runs in Claude" |
| "auto-fire", "fires your message", "sends automatically" | Walkoff never sends | "Claude drafts it in Klaviyo. You send it." |
| "done for you, start to finish", "triggers and approvals" | The marketer approves every send | "Setup done for you during early access" |
| "customers who care", "cheer for", "actually follow" | It knows where customers live | "customers in Chargers territory" |
| "converts well", "conversion lever" | No data behind it | Frost Buddy's result, as section 8 states it |
| "tracked to the order", "matched to your orders" | The brand's own Klaviyo UTMs and attribution do the tracking | "Shows up in the tracking you already use" |
| "encrypts your keys", "stored API credentials" | Walkoff holds no Klaviyo or Shopify keys | "Never asks for your keys" |
| "quiet hours", "frequency cap", "instant pause" | Not features | "Send timing" and "Smart Sending stays on" |
| "drop in your exports", "never leaves your computer" | Shopify brands set up in Claude with no files, and postal codes reach Walkoff, cut to areas on arrival | "No exports. Never a customer list." |
| "$5,689 in 7 minutes" | The time isn't defensible | "$5,689 in revenue" |

### Voice

- Short declaratives. Concrete nouns: products, colorways, territories, send
  times.
- Two beats, the second landing the point: "Their team wins. Your
  campaign's ready." "You approve. Every send." On slides, the second beat
  is set in the script face (section 17).
- Sentence case. Design can set headings in capitals.
- No em dashes, no en dashes and no exclamation marks. A middot (·) joins
  items in labels: "60 days free · No credit card".
- "Walkoff" in running text. The logo reads WALKOFF. with a period. Say "fan
  map", never "affinity map".

## 3. The story, beat by beat

Use this order for any deck. Sections 4 to 13 hold the facts behind each
beat.

1. **The moment.** Some wins you feel all week. The first brand in the inbox
   owns the moment.
2. **The problem.** Most brands can't act on it:
   - **No license.** They can't name the team, show its logo or say "Super
     Bowl", and most brands will never have a license.
   - **No warning.** Wins land at 4:41 on a Sunday afternoon, or at 11:35
     at night, and a result is worth acting on for about 30 hours.
   - **No map.** They don't know which team territories hold more of their
     customers than they'd expect. Walkoff's sample brand, in Southern
     California, has a following in Wisconsin it wouldn't have guessed.
   - **No time.** By hand, it means watching every game, picking products in
     the right colors, building a segment and writing careful copy, the same
     night.
3. **The insight.** You don't need the logo. Your catalog already has the
   colors. In the founder's words:

   > The biggest opportunity is brands that will never have a license. Say
   > the Chargers win. That night, an athleisure brand sends an email built
   > from its own catalog: the powder blues, the golds, the pieces that feel
   > like game day. It ties the brand to the moment and gives the message a
   > real reason to exist.
   >
   > Aaron, founder, Walkoff

4. **The product.** Walkoff watches every game, knows where a brand's
   customers are, and turns the big moments into ready Klaviyo campaigns
   built from the brand's own catalog, in Claude (sections 5 and 6).
5. **Day one.** The fan map: the team territories that hold more of a
   brand's customers than population predicts, and what to feature in each.
   Each standout is a segment to email the next time that team wins.
6. **Control.** The marketer approves every send. Never the whole list.
   Sends at a decent hour. Smart Sending stays on (section 12).
7. **Why now.** Claude now connects to Shopify and Klaviyo, so a marketer can
   do this work in one conversation. Walkoff runs next to those connectors:
   final whistle to a drafted Klaviyo campaign, with no new dashboard, no
   pixel, no API keys and no customer list.
8. **Proof.** Frost Buddy's early test (section 8).
9. **The model.** One flat price per brand, by leagues: $149 to $399 a month
   (section 9).
10. **What's next.** Sports first. Hometown moments already reach golf,
    tennis, racing and surf brands. Weather is **Next**; local events,
    holidays and culture, and trends are **Soon**, each included in the plan
    as it launches. SMS through Postscript and Attentive is **In progress**.

**The bigger idea, for investors:** Walkoff is a moment engine. It knows
what just happened, where a brand's customers are and what in the catalog
fits, and hands the marketer a finished campaign. Sports is the first kind
of moment. In one line: "Sports first. Then everything that moves your
customers."

## 4. Who it's for

**The buyer:** whoever owns email revenue at a direct-to-consumer brand on
Shopify and Klaviyo: an email or lifecycle manager, a head of retention, an
ecommerce director, a brand marketing lead, or the founder at a smaller
brand. Their customers cluster in a few sports markets, and they usually
have no team license. During early access, their team uses Claude or is
willing to.

**Who it serves**, as the site puts it:

| Who | The pitch |
|---|---|
| Brands without a license (the biggest opportunity) | Your customers have a team. When it wins, show up in its colors with products you already sell. |
| Licensed brands | Team products in front of the fans who buy the minute their team wins. |
| Golf, surf, tennis and racing brands | Your sport has no home team. It has hometowns. When someone from a town near your customers wins, that town gets its own campaign. |
| Agencies | One setup that works across every brand you run. |

**Best fit**, the way Walkoff's own outreach picks stores:

- On Shopify, in the US, with Klaviyo installed.
- Apparel first, and any catalog sold in colorways.
- Colorways close to their home teams' colors. Walkoff scores each store's
  public catalog for how good a three-product email in its home team's
  colors would look, from 0 to 1. A store at 0.6 or more qualifies.
- The person to reach: the founder, the head of retention, the lifecycle or
  email marketing lead, or the CMO.

**Not a fit yet:**

- Not on Klaviyo. Campaigns are built for Klaviyo. SMS through Postscript
  and Attentive is **In progress**.
- Won't use Claude. Claude is required during early access.
- Customers outside the US, Canada and the UK. Fan territories cover those
  three.

Not on Shopify is fine: a private upload page takes the brand's exports
instead.

**Hometown moments, in more detail.** A town's circle is the smallest of 3
to 25 miles that holds 75,000 people. A brand gets a town's moment when an
estimated 50 or more of its customers live in the circle, or 15 or more when
that's 2.5 times the town's share of people. One title makes many towns'
moments: Super Bowl LX's champion roster makes about 75. Each brand sees
only the few near its customers, up to 5 per event. The example: "San
Clemente, what a night." to customers within 5 miles of 92673, in the
champion's navy and green, with the brand's own products (section 17).

## 11. Why Walkoff

What Walkoff does that a brand can't easily do by hand, all true today:

1. **It starts from the brand's own customers.** The fan map compares the
   brand's customers in each team's territory with the population there, so
   the standouts are the brand's own, not a guess.
2. **It never misses the moment.** Every game, around the clock, in the
   queue about a minute after the final whistle. It knows the rivalries in
   every league, and where athletes are from.
3. **It turns a catalog into the right colors.** Claude reads every product,
   so a color named "Dune" counts too, and Walkoff picks one product per
   team color.
4. **It's built for brands without a license.** The copy rules ride along
   with every campaign.
5. **It works where marketers work.** In Claude, next to Shopify and
   Klaviyo. No new dashboard, no pixel, no keys.
6. **It's private by design.** Counts by area, never a customer list.
7. **The marketer stays in control.** Every send approved, never the whole
   list, never at 2 AM.

**What a brand can do today without Walkoff:** nothing; plan a few calendar
moments in advance, like an opening day or the big game; react by hand the
next day; or license the marks, which most brands never will. Named
competitors are a founder input (section 19). Don't name or characterize any

## 12. Data, privacy and control

**Data**

- **Counts, not customer lists.** From Shopify, Claude reads customer counts
  by postal code. Walkoff cuts each postal code to its area as it arrives,
  keeps only the area and the count, and refuses rows that hold names or
  emails. No names, emails or street addresses reach Walkoff, so there's no
  customer list to leak. Without Shopify, the upload page turns exports into
  counts in the browser before anything is sent.
- **No keys.** Walkoff never asks for Klaviyo or Shopify keys. The brand's
  own connectors in Claude do the work, on its own permissions.
- **Walled off.** Each brand's data is separate from every other brand's,
  checked on every request.
- **Sign-in** is a code emailed to an invited work address. No passwords.
  Walkoff keeps only hashed tokens.
- **Product details** go to Anthropic's API so Claude can tag the catalog.
  No customer data goes with them.
- **Athletes:** Walkoff keeps public facts only (team, position, where
  they're from). Athletes can ask Walkoff to correct or stop using them.

**Control** (the site's six, "You approve. Every send."):

| Control | What it means |
|---|---|
| Approval first | Every message waits for the marketer's yes. They see the moment, the audience and the message before anything sends. |
| Never your whole list | Each campaign goes to one segment: customers in one team's territory, or a circle around one town. |
| Send timing | Timed for the fans' time zone. A game that ends after 9 PM waits for 8 AM, never 2 AM. |
| Smart Sending on | Klaviyo's Smart Sending stays on, so nobody who just heard from the brand gets another email. |
| Copy rules | Without a license, copy never names the team, league, event or any athlete. The products in the team's colors do the talking. |
| Skip any moment | Not every win fits the brand. Skip it and it leaves the queue, or set triggers so only the wanted moments show up. |

Walkoff never sends anything. Klaviyo sends, from the brand's account, when
the marketer says so, with its unsubscribes and suppressions as they are.

## 18. Hard questions, for Q&A prep

For a founder preparing to answer, not for slides.

**Is this legal without a license?** Walkoff's copy rules keep a brand
without a license away from team marks: no team, league, player, stadium or
event names, no logos, no scores, and no hint of an endorsement. The brand
nods with the colors, and the city for pro teams, through its own products.
Counsel signed off on the hometown copy rules in October 2026. Walkoff's
rules lower the risk, but they aren't legal advice. `[FOUNDER: anything more
from counsel]`

**Why would a brand pay?** The win is the reason to email, and Walkoff does
the watching, the mapping, the picking and the drafting. The one result is
Frost Buddy's early test. The 60 days free let a brand try it on real wins.

**Won't brands over-email?** The default takes only big moments for a
brand's standout teams, and leaves out ordinary wins in sports that play most
days. Each send goes to one territory or one town, never the whole list, and
Klaviyo's Smart Sending stays on. Brands skip anything.

**Why Claude? What if a brand doesn't use it?** Claude is where the Shopify
and Klaviyo connectors are, so setup needs no files and drafting runs on the
brand's own Klaviyo permissions, with no keys for Walkoff to hold. Claude is
required during early access. Walkoff is built on MCP, the open standard
Claude's connectors use, and it's documented for Claude and Claude Code.
`[FOUNDER: plans for other assistants]`

**Where does the sports data come from?** ESPN's public scoreboards and
rosters, which are unofficial, plus Wikidata and Wikipedia for where
athletes are from and for surfing. The moment rules run on Walkoff's own
game state, not on any one feed's format, and every feed's health is public
at www.walkoff.io/status. `[FOUNDER: plans for licensed data]`

**What stops a bigger platform from building this?** `[FOUNDER]`. The facts

**How big can this get?** `[FOUNDER: market numbers]`. The levers: more

**Is MLB live?** The engine watches MLB today, and Frost Buddy's test ran on

## 20. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moment | Something that just happened, or is about to, that's worth an email: a walk-off, a comeback, a rivalry game, a title. |
| Trigger | A brand's rule for which moments fill its queue. Triggers fill the queue. They never send. |
| Queue | The moments to act on now, most urgent first, in Claude. |
| Fan territory | The area where most people follow a team. Where two teams share an area, its customers are split the way its fans are. |
| Lift | How many more of a brand's customers live in a territory than its population predicts. 2.4× means 2.4 times more. |
| Standout | A territory with a lift of at least 1.25×, at least 15 customers, and a gap too big to be chance. Up to 12 per brand. |
| Fan map | A brand's standouts, with what to feature in each. |
| Hometown moment | A moment for a town, when someone from there wins a title, a tournament or a race, or wins a playoff game at the very end. |
| Walk-off | Won it at the very end: the last at-bat, overtime, or a score in the last seconds. Also the name. |
| Licensed brand | A brand with the right to use a team's names and marks. Most brands aren't. |
| Early access | Walkoff's current stage. Walkoff sets up each brand and invites its team. |
| Connector | How Claude reaches an outside service, like Shopify, Klaviyo or Walkoff. |
